How to Choose a Preschool in Miami: A Parent's Checklist

By the Creativa Academy Team · November 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Miami-Dade County has hundreds of licensed early childhood programs. That's a lot to sort through — and without a clear framework, it's easy to end up overwhelmed, or choosing based on whoever had the nicest Instagram feed.

We've put together a practical checklist based on what early childhood research tells us actually matters, and what experienced Miami parents wish they had asked the first time around.

Start with the Non-Negotiables

Before you tour anything, filter your list by the basics:

License and accreditation. Every childcare program in Florida must be licensed by the Florida Department of Children and Families. You can verify any program's license at myflfamilies.com. Beyond the base license, look for Gold Seal accreditation — a Florida designation for providers that meet higher quality standards. Creativa Academy holds the Gold Seal quality designation.

Location and hours. No matter how great a school is, if it's 40 minutes from your house or closes before you can pick up, it's not workable. Set geographic and schedule boundaries before you tour anything.

Age appropriateness. Some schools serve infants through kindergarten; others specialize. Make sure the school you're considering has experience with your child's specific age group, and ask what the teacher-to-child ratios are for that age.

What to Look for During a Tour

A tour is worth more than any website. Here's what to pay attention to:

How do the teachers talk to children? Are they making eye contact and getting down to the child's level? Are they warm and calm even when things are chaotic? Teacher interaction style is the single most important variable in early childhood quality.

How do the children seem? Children in a healthy environment don't all look the same, but they generally look engaged, comfortable, and reasonably settled. A classroom full of children who look anxious or unoccupied is worth noting.

Is the environment rich and organized? Quality classrooms tend to have real materials (not just plastic toys), books within children's reach, clearly organized areas for different kinds of play, and work made by children displayed with care. Notice whether the space looks like it was designed for children to actually use it.

Is there outdoor time every day? Daily outdoor play is essential for young children's development. Ask how much time children spend outside and what that time looks like.

How do transitions happen? Preschool days involve a lot of transitions — from play to group time, from snack to outdoor time. How these are handled tells you a lot about the school's culture. Are transitions calm and predictable, or loud and rushed?

Questions to Ask the Director

Come with these questions and notice not just the answers, but how willingly and specifically they're answered:

  • What is your teacher turnover rate? (High turnover is a red flag in early childhood.)
  • What are your teacher-to-child ratios, and are they maintained throughout the day?
  • How do you communicate with parents — daily, weekly? Through an app or paper?
  • What is your discipline philosophy? How do you handle biting, hitting, or tantrums?
  • What does a typical day look like for children in my child's age group?
  • What training do your lead teachers have?
  • How do you handle separation anxiety for new students?
  • Are you VPK-approved? Do you work with School Readiness vouchers?

Red Flags to Watch Out For

These aren't necessarily deal-breakers on their own, but they're worth noting:

  • The director or lead teacher is not available to talk during your tour
  • You can't observe a classroom in session
  • Vague answers to direct questions about ratios, discipline, or curriculum
  • Heavy screen time (TV, tablets) during the school day
  • Classrooms that feel chaotic or understaffed
  • No outdoor play area, or outdoor time only "when the weather is nice"
  • No clear mechanism for daily parent communication

What Really Matters Most

After all the research and checklists, the families we talk to consistently say the same thing: they knew when they walked in. The feeling in the room. How a teacher bent down to talk to their child. Whether their child was comfortable enough to leave their side within ten minutes.

That instinct is based on something real. Quality in early childhood is fundamentally about relationships — between teacher and child, between school and family, between children and the environment. All the certifications and curricula in the world can't substitute for a classroom that simply feels warm, safe, and alive with curiosity.

Visit a few schools. Bring your child. Notice how they react. And then trust that combination of research and instinct together.

Checklist Summary

Before You Tour

  • Verify DCF license at myflfamilies.com
  • Check for Gold Seal accreditation
  • Confirm location and hours work for your schedule
  • Confirm they serve your child's age group

During the Tour

  • Observe how teachers talk to and interact with children
  • Notice whether children seem engaged and comfortable
  • Check that the environment is organized, real-material rich
  • Ask about daily outdoor time
  • Observe a transition between activities if possible

Ask the Director

  • Teacher-to-child ratios and turnover rate
  • Discipline philosophy
  • Parent communication method
  • VPK and School Readiness acceptance
  • How they support new students through separation
